Sample document: Understanding RAG chatbots

What is RAG?
RAG stands for Retrieval-Augmented Generation. A normal language model only knows what it learned during training. A RAG system lets the model use your own documents by first retrieving the relevant passages and then generating an answer from them. This is like an open-book exam: the model looks up the right pages before writing its answer.

What is a chunk?
A chunk is a small piece of a document, usually a few hundred characters long. Long documents are split into chunks because a model can only read a limited amount of text at once, and because searching small pieces gives more precise results. Neighbouring chunks overlap slightly so that no sentence is lost at the boundary.

What is an embedding?
An embedding is a list of numbers that captures the meaning of a piece of text. Texts with similar meaning get similar numbers, so the question "How do I recover my account?" can match a passage titled "Resetting your password" even though the words differ.

What is a vector database?
A vector database stores embeddings and quickly finds the ones closest to a question. Popular examples are FAISS, Chroma and Pinecone. For small projects, a simple matrix of numbers and a dot product is enough.

Why can a RAG chatbot still make mistakes?
If the retrieval step finds the wrong passages, the model answers from the wrong information. Scanned PDFs without a text layer cannot be read at all. Good chunk sizes and clear questions improve results.

What is offline mode in this project?
When no OpenAI API key is provided, this chatbot uses TF-IDF keyword search. It needs no internet and no payment, and it shows the most relevant passages from your document instead of a written answer.
